> ## Task
>
> ### Restaurant Waitlist App
>
> Time limit: 2 hours 30 minutes, including programming, checking your work, the README and submission. Your development tools, an emulator and your GitHub account must be installed and ready before the time starts.
>
> #### Background
>
> A restaurant gets very busy at weekends, and customers wait at the door without knowing their place in the queue. The owner wants a simple mobile app for the staff member at the door. Staff add each party to a waitlist on the phone, tell the party its ticket number, and manage the list as tables become free.
>
> #### Core criteria (Definition of Done)
>
> 1. Staff add a party by entering a name and the party size. The app gives the party a ticket number and adds it to the end of the waitlist.
> 2. The waitlist shows the waiting parties in the order they joined, and for each party how many parties are ahead of it. Staff can remove any party from the list, and the numbers update immediately.
> 3. If the app is closed completely and opened again, the waitlist is as it was and the ticket numbering continues from where it stopped.
>
> #### Three rules (so that nobody loses time on different assumptions)
>
> - The name cannot be empty, and the party size is a whole number greater than zero.
> - Every ticket number is unique and is never reused after a party is removed.
> - "Parties ahead" means the waiting parties before that party, not the number of people.
>
> #### General requirements
>
> 1. Technology: choose a mobile technology that suits the project, cross-platform or native.
> 2. Data storage: use a suitable on-device database or storage to keep the waitlist data.
> 3. Code organisation: keep the code clear and organised. No specific architecture and no automated tests are required.
> 4. Running on an emulator: the app must run on an Android emulator or an iOS simulator. A reviewer must be able to run it from the repository by following the steps in the README, without changing the code.
> 5. Documentation: a short README, as described under "What to submit".
> 6. GitHub: push the project to a GitHub repository. One push at the end is enough.
>
> #### Optional extras (really optional: the core criteria done well earn full marks)
>
> - Edit the name or the size of a waiting party.
> - Undo the last removal.
> - A history screen of the removed parties.
> - An estimated waiting time for each party.
>
> #### Out of scope
>
> - A backend server, user accounts or login.
> - A separate app for customers.
> - SMS or any other notifications.
> - Publishing to an app store.
> - Support for both platforms. One is enough.
> - Support for more than one branch.
> - Visual design: a simple, clear interface that is easy to use on a phone is enough, and its looks are not evaluated.
>
> #### Rules
>
> - You may use any AI tool in this stage, and the internet is available.
> - You may ask questions to clarify the requirements. Every clarification is given to all candidates.
> - Priority goes to the core criteria and their quality. Start the optional extras only after the core criteria are complete.
